# Data sources and methodology

Bible Atlas AI treats its structured data as the source of truth: the interface, the search and any future AI feature may only show what is recorded here. This document explains where each kind of information comes from, how it was checked, and what remains uncertain.

At a glance: 101 places, 117 people, 222 events, 15 journeys and collections, and 933 distinct Scripture references backed by 2,596 verses of the World English Bible. Every count shown in the app is computed from the data.

## Scripture text

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Translation | World English Bible (WEB), 2020 stable text edition |
| Source | eBible.org, verse-per-line edition (`eng-web_vpl.xml`), downloaded October 2026 |
| License | Public domain ("World English Bible" is a trademark of eBible.org) |
| File in the app | `src/data/scripture/web.json` (2,596 verses from 18 books: the four Gospels, Acts, and Paul's letters from Romans to Philemon), generated by `npm run data:scripture` |

- Only verses the atlas references are extracted. That includes references written out in notes, such as "(John 14:22)". Text is copied verbatim; the only processing is trimming surrounding whitespace and decoding XML entities. The WEB's own punctuation (curly quotes, em dashes, non-breaking spaces) is kept.
- eBible.org asks that a changed text not be called the World English Bible. The atlas never alters it, and every quotation is shown with its reference and the WEB label.
- Verses the WEB leaves empty (for example Acts 15:34, a textual variant) are not displayed.
- **Tests:**
  - A unit test fails if any reference in the atlas lacks its verses in the generated file. Regenerating the file from the source (`npm run data:scripture`) reproduces it exactly.
  - Every short quotation on a journey stop (72 of the 190 stops have one) must appear word for word in its verse, inside the passage the stop cites.
  - Any words in curly quotation marks, in the data or in interface text that cites a verse, must appear in the verses cited. Curly quotes are reserved for Scripture.
- Links to read a passage in context go to the WEB on eBible.org.

## Places and coordinates

Each place lists its coordinate sources; the first is the **primary source** and its coordinates are used as published.

| Source | Primary source for | License |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [OpenBible.info Bible Geocoding Data](https://github.com/openbibleinfo/Bible-Geocoding-Data) | 93 places: identification, coordinates and confidence score | CC BY 4.0 |
| [Pleiades](https://pleiades.stoa.org/) | 5 regions where OpenBible.info's point coincides with a city or is missing: Macedonia, Achaia ("Greece"), Phoenicia, Syria, Cilicia. Also cross-reference ids | CC BY 3.0 |
| [Wikidata](https://www.wikidata.org/) | 3 places neither dataset has as a separate record: the Temple Mount, the wilderness of Judea (Judaean Desert) and the hill country of Judea (Judaean Mountains). Also cross-reference ids | CC0 |

OpenBible.info's dataset draws on more than seventy modern Bible atlases, dictionaries, encyclopedias and commentaries, records every proposed identification, and scores how confident current scholarship is in each.

**Verification.** `npm run data:verify` re-reads the OpenBible.info dataset (and queries Pleiades and Wikidata for the places that use them) and checks every coordinate against its primary source. At the time of writing all 101 places match their source exactly, and every certainty label agrees with its score or has a recorded reason.

**Certainty labels.** Labels come from OpenBible.info's location score (its `modern_associations` score, which combines the confidence that a place is identified with a site and that the site is located correctly):

| Label | Rule | Count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identified site | Score of 800 or more | 72 |
| Probable identification | 500–799, or an alternative site is still proposed | 5 |
| Location debated | Below 500 with competing candidates; the marker shows the leading candidate | 5 |
| Traditional site | Located by long-standing tradition (set by hand) | 3 |
| Approximate location | A region; the marker is a representative point (set by hand) | 16 |

The 21 hand-set labels and their reasons are listed in `scripts/verify-places.ts`:

- Rhodes and Derbe;
- three traditional sites: the Jordan River baptism site, Gethsemane and Golgotha;
- sixteen regions: Macedonia, Greece, Egypt, Samaria, Arabia, Phoenicia, Syria, Cilicia, Galilee, the wilderness of Judea, the hill country of Judea, the country of the Gadarenes, the Decapolis, Gennesaret, Pontus and Asia.

The verification script fails if any other label disagrees with its score.

**Modern locations** come from OpenBible.info's modern site names where those are in common use (for example Konya, Kavala, Veria, Pozzuoli), and otherwise from general geographic reference ("ruins of Perge, near Antalya"). Contested areas use neutral geographic descriptors (West Bank, Golan Heights, Jerusalem) rather than national labels.

**Map positions are approximate.** A marker shows the identified site or settlement; it does not mean an event happened at that exact spot. Islands are marked at a representative point, and regions at the point the primary source gives for the region.

**Home towns.** Six places are on the map only because the text gives them as someone's home: Cyrene (Simon and Lucius), Arimathaea (Joseph), Thyatira (Lydia), Alexandria (Apollos), Pontus (Aquila) and Asia (Tychicus and Trophimus). In all, 28 people have a home town or region recorded, each with the verse that states it, such as "a man of Pontus by race" (Acts 18:2).

A home is recorded only where the text says where someone is from or lives. Mary Magdalene, for example, is not placed at Magdala: Luke says only that she "was called Magdalene".

## Journeys and collections

Route journeys were built by reading the narrative in the WEB and recording each place in the order the text gives it.

- **Routes (6).** Paul's first, second and third journeys, his arrest and trials, his voyage to Rome, and Philip the evangelist in Acts 8.
- **Collections (9).** The birth and childhood of Jesus, John the Baptist, the ministry of Jesus, the final week, the resurrection appearances, women in the Gospels and Acts, Jerusalem in Acts, Peter and the early church, and Paul's conversion and early ministry.
- **Collections are not routes.** They gather places the text does not connect into an itinerary. They are never drawn as lines, and each explains how it is ordered.
- **Stop order** follows the narrative. Unit tests pin Paul's second journey to exactly 18 stops in the order of Acts 15:36–18:22, and check that every route's stops follow the order of the text.
- **Leg mode** is `sea` only where the text says the travelers sailed, `land` only where it says they went overland (Acts 13:6, 20:13), and otherwise `unspecified`.
- **Leg certainty** is `stated`, `implied` (the text implies but does not name the destination) or `uncertain` (the course is not described).
- **Lines are schematic.** They connect documented stops with gentle curves; they do not trace historical roads or sea lanes.
- **Mapped distance** is the sum of straight-line distances between consecutive stops, labelled "approximate mapped distance".
- **Who a journey is "with"** is computed from the events at its stops: on a route, everyone the text names at three or more stops. Paul's second journey is with Paul, Silas, Timothy, Aquila and Priscilla; Priscilla and Aquila sail with Paul from Cenchreae to Ephesus (Acts 18:18–19). A thematic collection can limit its people to one group: the women's collection lists only women.

Decisions worth knowing:

| Situation | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Tarsus on the second journey | Not included. Acts 15:41 names only "Syria and Cilicia"; no town is named before Derbe. |
| Lystra to Troas (Acts 16:6–8) | Drawn as uncertain: Acts traces the leg only by regions (Phrygia and Galatia, past Mysia). |
| Philippi (Acts 16:12) | Characterized in the text's words: "a city of Macedonia, the foremost of the district, a Roman colony". The atlas does not call it Paul's first ministry in Europe. |
| "Went up and greeted the assembly" (Acts 18:22) | Mapped to Jerusalem as an **implied** stop; the city is not named. |
| Amphipolis and Apollonia (Acts 17:1) | Mapped as stops the travelers passed through. A search for "after Philippi" names all three places Acts 17:1 gives for the move. |
| Athens: by sea or overland? (Acts 17:14–15) | Mode left unspecified; the text does not say. |
| "Greece" (Acts 20:2) | Mapped as a region at a representative point for Achaia, not at Corinth, which Acts does not name here. |
| Trogyllium (Acts 20:15) | Included because the WEB names it; noted as absent from many modern translations. |
| Departure for Rome (Acts 27:1–2) | Mapped from Caesarea, where Paul had been held (Acts 25:4–6); noted as implied. |
| The storm (Acts 27:13–44) | The leg to Malta is drawn as uncertain; the ship's course is unknown. |
| Market of Appius and Three Taverns (Acts 28:15) | Ordered by their positions on the road to Rome; noted as implied. |
| Mary's visit to Elizabeth and John's birth (Luke 1:39–80) | Mapped to "the hill country of Judea" as a region; Luke names only "a city of Judah". The traditional site, Ein Karem, is mentioned as tradition and not marked. |
| Bethany beyond the Jordan (John 1:28) | Mapped to the Jordan River marker, which already shows the leading candidate (Al-Maghtas); the site is debated, as the note says. |
| The country of the Gadarenes (Mark 5:1) | A district: the marker is the city of Gadara (Umm Qais). The note says the event happened by the shore, gives Matthew's "Gergesenes" and the manuscripts' "Gerasenes", and calls Kursi a traditional site. |
| The resurrection appearances | Grouped by setting. The Gospels and Acts differ in detail, and the accounts are not merged. |

## People

- **Who is included.** 117 people, each named or clearly described in the WEB, each connected to at least one mapped event, and each with Scripture references.
- **Unnamed people stay unnamed.** They have descriptive names taken from the text, such as "the Samaritan woman", "the woman with a flow of blood" and "the disciple whom Jesus loved". Later traditional names are not used as names.
- **The original Twelve.** All twelve are separate people, listed in the order of Matthew 10:2–4. A unit test checks there are exactly twelve.
- **Matthias** is a separate person, not counted among the original Twelve. Acts 1:26 says he "was counted with the eleven apostles".
- **Thaddaeus.**
  - The display name follows Matthew 10:3 and Mark 3:18.
  - His other names are Lebbaeus, Judas son of James (Luke 6:16; Acts 1:13) and Judas (not Iscariot) (John 14:22). The note explains that the atlas follows the common reading that these name one apostle.
  - The identification with Jude, author of the Epistle of Jude, is labelled "traditional and uncertain" and is not assumed. "Jude" is not one of his names or search terms, and a test checks this.
- **Names and identification notes.** 43 people have a note, for example:
  - Herod the Great, Herod the tetrarch, Herod Agrippa I and King Agrippa are kept apart;
  - Levi is identified with Matthew, following the common reading;
  - Bartholomew and Nathanael, and John son of Zebedee and the beloved disciple, are kept separate;
  - Luke's authorship of Acts and the "we" passages is given as tradition.
- **Directory groups.** The People directory groups people as lenses for discovery: Jesus and the Twelve, Paul and his companions, women, the early church, rulers and officials, others in the Gospels, others in Acts. The groups are not claims that these people formed a group, and a person can appear in more than one.
- **Counts and connections are derived.** A person's events, places and journeys, and the people connected to them, are computed from the events.
- **Timothy** is connected only where a passage names him:
  - Lystra (Acts 16:1–2);
  - the second-journey stops from Troas to Neapolis, Berea, Corinth, Athens and Thessalonica (Acts 16–18; 1 Thessalonians 3:1–6);
  - Ephesus and Macedonia (Acts 19:22; 1 Corinthians 4:17; 1 Timothy 1:3);
  - Philippi (Philippians 1:1; 2:19–24).

  No home town is recorded for him: Acts 16:1 says only that he "was there" when Paul came to Derbe and Lystra.

## Events and summaries

- **Events.** 222 events, each citing the passage that narrates it. Many have a shorter excerpt shown first.
  - Where the Gospels tell the same event, the event cites one account and lists the others as related references (67 events). Accounts are not merged.
  - 95 events carry a location note, where the text is vaguer than the map (a region rather than a town, an unnamed house) or the site is debated.
  - An event's people are those the passage names or clearly includes. Groups ("the eleven", "the women") are not expanded into named individuals unless the passage lists them.
- **How the content was drafted.** Summaries, roles and notes are **editorial paraphrases**, drafted with AI assistance:
  - The expansion (Gospels, early Acts, later Acts, and further places) was drafted in parallel. Each draft was checked by a script before merging: the atlas validator, every reference against the WEB, every quotation verbatim, every new place against OpenBible.info's coordinate and score band, and unique names.
  - The original content was audited verse by verse in an earlier review. The expanded atlas then had a separate content review of each major area (the four journeys of Paul, the life of Jesus, the new Acts content, the new people and the new collections). Its corrections have been applied.
- **Automated content tests** check:
  - verbatim quotations, valid references and coordinate provenance;
  - that relationships agree in both directions and that every displayed participant comes from the events;
  - that every search answer cites Scripture.

## Dates

Acts and the Gospels give no calendar dates, so periods are shown as scholarly estimates with a note, and only where a responsible estimate exists:

| Journey | Label | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First journey | c. AD 46–49 | A common estimate; some chronologies date it earlier |
| Second journey | c. AD 49–52 | Anchored by Gallio, proconsul of Achaia (Acts 18:12), whose term an inscription at Delphi places around AD 51–52 |
| Third journey | c. AD 53–57 | Mid-50s, ending around AD 57; some chronologies use AD 54–58 |
| Voyage to Rome | c. AD 59–60 | Arrival in Rome about AD 60 |
| Birth of Jesus | c. 6–4 BC | "In the days of King Herod" (Matthew 2:1); Herod's death is usually dated to 4 BC, though some argue for 1 BC |
| Final week | c. AD 30–36 | The range within which scholars generally date the crucifixion |

These follow the summaries of scholarly chronology in the English Wikipedia articles *Paul the Apostle*, *Gallio inscription*, *Herod the Great* and *Chronology of Jesus*, and other published chronologies of Paul (consulted October 2026). They are presented only as approximate. The newer journeys and collections carry no period.

## Known limitations

These are worth a human expert's review before the atlas is used for teaching:

1. **Summaries are AI-drafted.** They were checked against the text by automated tests and separate reviews, but a review by a qualified biblical scholar has not yet taken place.
2. **Some modern descriptions** ("near Hatunsaray", "near Gelemiş", "Behramkale") come from general geographic reference rather than the cited datasets.
3. **Region markers** (Macedonia, Greece, Egypt, Galilee, the Decapolis, Pontus, Asia and the others) are conventions, not locations. Three representative points (the Temple Mount and the two Judaean regions) come from Wikidata because the Bible datasets have no separate record.
4. **Debated sites** (Bethsaida, Emmaus, Enon, Ephraim, Arimathaea) show only the leading candidate; the alternatives are named in the notes, not mapped.
5. **Traditional sites** (Gethsemane's garden, the Church of the Holy Sepulchre, Al-Maghtas) reflect tradition rather than archaeological certainty, and are labelled that way.
6. **Ships that "came opposite"** a place (Chios, Cnidus, Salmone, Cauda) are marked at the island or city, not the ship's offshore position.
7. **Not every event is mapped.** Events the text does not place are left out, and the collections say so. Examples: the Last Supper's house (mapped to Jerusalem itself), the transfiguration, the call of Philip and Nathanael (John 1:43–51), and John the Baptist's imprisonment and death.
8. **Identifications follow the common reading where the text implies them.** Examples: Thaddaeus with Judas son of James, Levi with Matthew, the James of Acts 15 with the Lord's brother. Each is stated in a note rather than hidden.
9. **Dates are estimates** from secondary summaries of scholarly chronology.
10. **No Old Testament content yet.** The optional Old Testament pilot was not started; the atlas covers the Gospels, Acts and Paul's letters.
